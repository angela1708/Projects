Technology has come a long way in the first quarter of the 21st century. Every day now, you see people with phones, smartwatches, computers, and electronics that are no longer tactile. Technology has always been a thing. Since the 20th century, when we've seen the first inventions of what we use today, everything back then wasn't touch-sensitive. Phones back then were full of buttons and connected through a landline. Computers used to not have an internet explorer, and did not run everything on one machine, let alone be portable. Things change, and these inventions were revolutionized. We chose this topic as we're interested in seeing how the future may turn out. What we know and use today may seem to be the future, but in the true future, we could have technology implanted into our brains to communicate more easily or work better. So far, what we know of the cars could go from running on an electric battery to flying with an electric battery. It's really fascinating to see where technology could go, but we cannot forget where it started. As we grow older, we're stuck in a loop of nostalgia from our young days of how we used to have MP3 players for music, to MP3 players no longer being used as they have been incorporated into online streaming access through phones being easier.

Looking back at human history, major inventions have always marked turning points in human progress. In 2007, Apple Inc. transformed the mobile phone industry with the release of the first iPhone. Its touch-sensitive, all-screen design changed how people communicated, accessed information, and used technology daily. The iPhone set new standards for smartphones and influenced countless devices that followed. However, the earliest major and widely recorded invention dates back much further. In the 14th century, there was the invention of the printing press was invented. In the 16th century, there was the invention of the telescope. In the 19th century, there was the invention of the car. To name a few, these inventions created many years ago have always been undergoing change. The printing press helped create books, texts, and newspapers. The telescope allowed us to see far beyond our planet, into the stars, and analyze space. The car changed the way we travel, going from point A to point B the quickest way possible. Point being, these inventions played a major role in helping the human race grow and progress by generating new ideas and opportunities. Each invention was built upon previous knowledge, encouraging creativity, problem-solving, and innovation across generations. As we discovered better ways to share information, explore the universe, and travel efficiently, society began to advance at a faster pace. These inventions not only improved daily life but also inspired future inventors to push boundaries even further. By solving existing challenges, they opened doors to new questions and possibilities, leading to even more breakthroughs. Overall, inventions serve as stepping stones for human development, showing how ideas evolve and continue to shape the world we live in today.
